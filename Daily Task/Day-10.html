Engines




Car engines are grouped by how they use fuel and how their cylinders are arranged, with
the main types being petrol engines, diesel engines, and electric motors. Common
layouts include inline, V-type, and flat configurations.


Fuel and Power Types


  • Petrol (Gasoline) Engines: Use spark plugs to burn fuel and air; smooth and quiet,
    common in small and mid-size cars.
  • Diesel Engines: Use high pressure and heat to ignite fuel without spark plugs; loud
    but powerful and fuel-efficient for long drives.
  • Electric Motors: Run entirely on battery power with no fuel or emissions; quiet and
    quick to accelerate.
  • Hybrid Systems: Combine a fuel engine with an electric motor to save fuel.



Cylinder Layouts




  • Inline / Straight: Cylinders sit in a single straight line; simple to build and very smooth
    (like 3, 4, or 6-cylinder setups).
  • V-Engine: Cylinders are arranged in two rows forming a V-shape; fits more cylinders in
    less space for high power (like V6 or V8).
  • Flat / Boxer: Cylinders lie flat on opposite sides facing each other; gives the car a low
    center of gravity for better balance.
  • Uses a rotating triangle shape instead of standard up-and-down
    pistons; small and light.

Forced Induction Variations


Engines can also be classified by how they breathe to generate power:


Naturally Aspirated (NA):
Rely purely on standard atmospheric pressure to pull air
into the combustion chamber. They offer highly predictable power delivery and
straightforward maintenance.



Turbocharged / Supercharged:
Use an exhaust turbine (turbo) or an engine-driven
belt (supercharger) to force-feed extra air into the engine. This allows small, fuel-conscious
engines to punch well above their weight class in performance
